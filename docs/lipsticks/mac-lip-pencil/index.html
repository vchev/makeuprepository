Titanium dioxideUnclear
  • Skin: No concerns found.
  • Swallowed: Unclear. EFSA's food additive panel could not rule out a genotoxicity concern for swallowed TiO2 particles and concluded E171 can no longer be considered safe as a food additive. This is about food, which is swallowed in far larger amounts than lipstick; it shows the oral evidence is unsettled, not that lipstick causes harm. Allowed by the US FDA: Listed as safe for coloring "cosmetics" (not limited to "externally applied" cosmetics, which 21 CFR 70.3(v) defines as excluding the lips); includes eye area, at good manufacturing practice levels.

How to read this

Each ingredient is rated on its own for the ways a lip liner reaches you: on skin and lips and swallowed. The list shows the more cautious of the ratings, and "not yet researched" counts as more cautious than "no concerns found".

A rating is about the ingredient at typical cosmetic use. It is not a verdict on this product.

Ingredient lists can change. Always check the label on your product.

The Lip Ledger rates ingredients, not products. Ratings are produced automatically by fixed rules from full safety reports and regulations, and have not yet been reviewed by an independent toxicologist. Ingredient lists can change: always check the label on your product. Brand and product names are used only to identify products; The Lip Ledger is not affiliated with any brand.
