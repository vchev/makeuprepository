On skin and lips

Unclear

Why: Harm reported only in limited, high-dose or inconclusive evidence.

  • 2026Could not decide

    The US FDA said the public record does not yet hold enough safety data to call sunscreens with this ingredient generally recognized as safe, and asked for more studies, partly because people now use far more sunscreen than when it was first reviewed. The FDA stressed that this is not a finding that the ingredient is unsafe.

    “The 2021 proposed order (OTC000008) does not represent a conclusion by the FDA that the sunscreen active ingredients proposed as having insufficient data are unsafe for use in sunscreens.”

    US FDA, Questions and Answers: FDA's regulatory actions on over-the-counter sunscreen (covers OTC Monograph M020, the 2021 proposed order OTC000008 and the 2026 final orders)

  • 2020Could not decide

    An FDA clinical trial in 48 adults found this ingredient passes through the skin into the blood above the level at which the FDA asks for extra safety studies. Sunscreen was applied to 75% of the body four times a day, far more than a lip balm. Absorption is not proof of harm, and the authors said people should keep using sunscreen.

    “all 6 of the tested active ingredients administered in 4 different sunscreen formulations were systemically absorbed and had plasma concentrations that surpassed the FDA threshold for potentially waiving some of the additional safety studies for sunscreens. These findings do not indicate that individuals should refrain from the use of sunscreen.”

    Matta MK, Florian J, Zusterzeel R, et al. Effect of Sunscreen Application on Plasma Concentration of Sunscreen Active Ingredients: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. 2020;323(3):256-267

  • 2026Allowed · US FDA

    Permitted sunscreen active ingredient under OTC Monograph M020 (the 2020 deemed final order)

    US FDA, Questions and Answers: FDA's regulatory actions on over-the-counter sunscreen (covers OTC Monograph M020, the 2021 proposed order OTC000008 and the 2026 final orders)
