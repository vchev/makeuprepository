Ingredients/Titanium dioxide
Ingredient

Titanium dioxide

In lip products:Unclear

Lip products reach you two ways: through skin contact, and the small amount you swallow. The rating above is the more cautious of the two. Ratings for other ways people are exposed apply to other kinds of products.

INCI name
TITANIUM DIOXIDE
CAS number

Used as
colorant, opacifying
Also called
titanium(IV) oxide, E171

On skin and lips

No concerns found

Why: Approved for this use by a regulator (US FDA).

  • Allowed · US FDA

    Listed as safe for coloring "cosmetics" (not limited to "externally applied" cosmetics, which 21 CFR 70.3(v) defines as excluding the lips); includes eye area, at good manufacturing practice levels

    US 21 CFR 73.2575 Titanium dioxide (color additive listing for cosmetics)

Swallowed

Unclear

Why: Harm reported only in limited, high-dose or inconclusive evidence.

  • 2021Could not decide

    EFSA's food additive panel could not rule out a genotoxicity concern for swallowed TiO2 particles and concluded E171 can no longer be considered safe as a food additive. This is about food, which is swallowed in far larger amounts than lipstick; it shows the oral evidence is unsettled, not that lipstick causes harm.

    “a concern for genotoxicity could not be ruled out”

    EFSA Panel on Food Additives and Flavourings. Safety assessment of titanium dioxide (E171) as a food additive. EFSA Journal 19(5):6585

  • Allowed · US FDA

    Listed as safe for coloring "cosmetics" (not limited to "externally applied" cosmetics, which 21 CFR 70.3(v) defines as excluding the lips); includes eye area, at good manufacturing practice levels

    US 21 CFR 73.2575 Titanium dioxide (color additive listing for cosmetics)

  • 2024Not counted

    We could only read a summary of this document, not the full text, so it does not count toward the rating.

    The SCCS could not rule out genotoxicity for most TiO2 grades in products that may be swallowed, and did not recommend safe limits for oral exposure.

    “the SCCS cannot recommend any safe limits”

    SCCS, Scientific Advice on Titanium dioxide (TiO2), SCCS/1661/23

Other ways people are exposed (for powders, sprays and eye products)

Breathed in

Evidence of harm

Why: Restricted for safety in EU.

  • 2010Found harm

    IARC classified TiO2 as possibly carcinogenic to humans (Group 2B) based on lung tumours in rats exposed by inhalation; evidence in humans inadequate.

    IARC Monographs Volume 93: Carbon Black, Titanium Dioxide, and Talc

  • 2021Restricted · EU

    Powder form with >=1% particles <=10 um: only loose face powder (max 25%) and hair aerosol spray (max 1.4%)

    Commission Regulation (EU) 2021/850 amending Annexes II-VI to Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009
