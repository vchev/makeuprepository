How we rate

Every rating on this site is produced automatically by the same fixed rules. Nobody picks a rating by hand, and no brand pays to be listed or rated.

We rate ingredients, not products

Each ingredient gets a rating for each way people are exposed to it: on the skin and lips, swallowed, breathed in, and near the eyes. For a lipstick we look at skin contact and swallowing, and show the more cautious of the two. We never give a product an overall safe or unsafe verdict.

The five ratings

For each ingredient and route, the first rule that matches decides the rating.

  1. Evidence of harm The EU, US, California or Canada banned or restricted it for safety; or the International Agency for Research on Cancer put it in Group 1 or 2A for this route; or a regulator, an expert panel or a human study found harm at the amounts used in cosmetics.
  2. Known allergen A regulator lists it as a cosmetic allergen, or human patch-test or clinical studies show allergy.
  3. Unclear Harm or uncertain results appear only in animal or lab studies, at doses far above cosmetic use, or the experts said the data were not enough to decide.
  4. No concerns found Reliable studies or full safety assessments looked and found no harm for this route, or a regulator approved it for this use (for example, a US FDA color additive listing that covers lip products).
  5. No research found We have nothing reliable on record for this route yet. This means the research is missing or we have not reached it. It is not a sign of harm.

Which evidence counts

  • Full texts only. We count a report or paper only if we read the whole document. An abstract or a summary is not enough. When a full text is paywalled or blocked, we list the source as "not counted". The one exception is a cancer classification from the International Agency for Research on Cancer, where the official classification itself is the finding.
  • Reputable sources only. Regulations and opinions from government safety bodies count. Individual studies count only if they are peer-reviewed and rated reliable on the Klimisch scale used by EU and OECD chemical assessors.
  • Expert panel reports. Many ratings come from the Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) Expert Panel, which reads the published research on an ingredient in full. The panel is funded by the cosmetics industry, and we note this wherever we cite it. We record a CIR conclusion only when the report names the ingredient in its conclusion.
  • Swallowing. A CIR conclusion counts for swallowing when the full report includes lip-product uses, where small amounts are swallowed.

What we do not use

We do not use rating apps, scores from advocacy groups, social media, or brand marketing as evidence. Those can point us to a question, but the answer has to come from the primary research or a regulator.

Limits

  • Our rules and ratings have not yet been reviewed by an independent toxicologist.
  • Dose matters. A substance can be harmful at high doses and harmless at the tiny amounts in a lipstick. Our ratings follow what the sources concluded about cosmetic use.
  • Ingredient lists come from each brand's official website on the date shown. Brands change formulas, so the label on your product is what counts.
  • Many ingredients have no full safety report we can read yet. As we add sources, ratings can change.
