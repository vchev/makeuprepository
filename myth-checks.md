# Myth checks

<p class="lede">These claims come from a 2026 r/nontoxic thread asking for "non-toxic" mascara and lipstick. Several are accurate; others mix up hazard and risk.</p>

| Claim | Verdict | What the evidence says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional mascaras use carbon black (CI 77266), clean ones use black iron oxide (CI 77499), so check the CI numbers | **Accurate, but not a safety filter** | Both are approved, low-risk pigments. [More]({{ '/ingredients/carbon-black-vs-iron-oxide/' | relative_url }}) |
| Titanium dioxide is a possible carcinogen only as inhaled powder, so it's a smaller concern in lipstick | **Partly right** | Inhalation is the route behind the cancer flag. But in 2024 the EU's scientific committee couldn't rule out a genotoxicity concern for swallowed TiO2, which is the lipstick route. [More]({{ '/ingredients/titanium-dioxide/' | relative_url }}) |
| IARC Volume 93 covers carbon black and titanium dioxide | **Accurate** | Volume 93 (2010) covers carbon black, titanium dioxide and talc |
| Check older drugstore formulas for parabens and formaldehyde releasers | **Reasonable, for allergy** | The documented risk from formaldehyde-releasing preservatives in makeup is skin allergy. Parabens have comparatively low allergy rates |
| Anything scented is toxic because "fragrance" hides undisclosed chemicals | **Overstated** | Fragrance is a leading allergen, a good reason to avoid it if you react. Not naming the blend is trade-secret law, not proof of toxicity. [More]({{ '/ingredients/fragrance/' | relative_url }}) |
| Taking TiO2 lip color means it's constantly ingested | **Accurate on the route** | Lip products are partly swallowed over the day, in small amounts |
| Mascara made in Europe is safer | **Partly right** | The EU bans and restricts more cosmetic ingredients than the US, but most of those are never used in makeup. The specific product matters more than where it's made |
| Put every ingredient into EWG's database to see how toxic a product is | **Misleading** | Hazard databases flag whether a concern exists anywhere in the literature, not whether the dose in a product matters. Use them for questions, not verdicts. See [how we work]({{ '/methodology/' | relative_url }}) |
| "Clean" brands have some poorly rated products and mainstream brands have some good ones | **Accurate** | Brand labels don't track ingredient risk product by product |

<p class="muted">Claims about specific brands, such as which ones use lake dyes, will be checked against current ingredient lists as brand profiles are added.</p>
