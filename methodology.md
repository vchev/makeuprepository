# How we work

<p class="lede">Every ingredient page answers two questions separately: can this substance cause harm under some condition (hazard), and does it, at the dose and route a makeup user actually gets (risk).</p>

## Sources, strongest first

1. **Regulatory risk assessments** that weigh all the available data: opinions from the EU's Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety (SCCS), the EU Cosmetics Regulation [1223/2009](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2009/1223/oj) and its annexes, FDA rules, Health Canada, and the European Food Safety Authority for ingredients that are swallowed.
2. **Hazard classifications** such as the [IARC Monographs](https://monographs.iarc.who.int/list-of-classifications/) and EU chemical classifications. These say what a substance *can* do, often by one route only.
3. **Peer-reviewed studies**, weighted by design. Human studies and systematic reviews count for more than single cell or animal studies.
4. **Brand disclosures and ingredient lists**, checked against the sources above.
5. **Rating apps, databases and social media**, including EWG Skin Deep, Yuka and Think Dirty. We treat these as claims to check. Their scores mostly reflect whether a hazard has been reported somewhere, not whether the amount in a product matters.

Where a page relies on a secondary source, such as a law firm's summary of a court ruling, we say so and replace it with the primary document when we can.

## How verdicts are worded

- **Low concern:** no good evidence of harm at the levels and routes found in makeup.
- **Real risk for some people:** a documented harm, usually allergy, that applies to a subset of users.
- **Open question:** the evidence can't yet rule out a concern for a specific route or product type. We say what's missing rather than calling it safe or toxic.

## Updates and corrections

Every ingredient page shows when it was last reviewed. If you spot an error or a newer source, please [open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/vchev/makeuprepository/issues).

This site is general information, not medical advice.
